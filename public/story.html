Checkout can say completed while the charge is declined.

This holds your files until the money clears. Capture Desk sits beside PayPal checkout, so a freelancer can take the card payment and still hold the files until the capture clears.

Use case

A freelancer takes a card payment for work that is not delivered yet. On the desk the job is Freelance delivery: final files, and a sandbox charge is USD 25.00.

The buyer pays first. The files stay put until the capture clears. Reading only “order completed” is how unpaid work starts.

Work has not started.

The files stay locked on a decline.

New checkout is the only next charge.

The split

PayPal can return both of these in the checkout response. They are not the same answer.

Order

COMPLETED

at checkout

while

Capture

DECLINED

the charge did not clear

The desk shows those two lines from the checkout response. A later read of the same order can say CREATED. That later word stays off the screen.

The flow

  1. New checkout

    Pick a test card and press New checkout. The desk shows Checking with PayPal…

  2. Read both lines

    Order COMPLETED at checkout. Capture DECLINED. The job stays on Work not started. Files locked.

  3. Tell the buyer

    The desk writes one line. Insufficient funds uses the 5120 sentence. Suspected fraud, lost or stolen, and an invalid or restricted card use the do-not-retry sentence with 9500, 9520, or 5180.

  4. A later checkout can clear

    Press New checkout again. Sandbox Buyer · clears when the capture is completed. The screen says Work can start and Files unlocked, then the two status lines.

0500, 5400, 5120, 1330, and 5100 use “The charge didn't clear. PayPal response code” and that code. 9500, 9520, and 5180 use “Do not retry this card. PayPal response code” and that code.

Gemma gemma-4-31b-it proposes fulfill and retry. The desk writes the buyer line for all eight codes. A hard guard overwrites the model. A declined capture stays unfulfilled, so the files stay locked.
